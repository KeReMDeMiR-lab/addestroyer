# addestroyer privacy policy

Effective 13 November 2026.

addestroyer is a Chrome extension that removes ads from the pages you visit
by playing a short animation of a pixel-art fighter destroying them. It does
not collect, send, sell or share any information about you.

## What it stores

addestroyer keeps these on your own computer, in the browser's storage for
the extension:

- your settings: whether it is on, whether animations are on, the speed, the
  fighter and weapon you picked, and the sites where you switched it off
- how many ads it has destroyed in total
- for each site where you used "Undo last kill", which parts of the page you
  brought back, so they are left alone next time

Nothing else is stored.

## What it looks at

To find ads, addestroyer looks at the structure of each page you open: the
names and sizes of the elements on it. This happens inside the page and the
result is not kept.

Some weapons slice, melt or swallow an ad. An ad usually comes from another
company's server, and the browser does not let an extension copy how such an
ad looks, so for these weapons addestroyer takes a picture of the tab you are
looking at and cuts the ad out of it. The picture is taken only at the moment
one of these weapons is about to hit an ad, is used for that one animation,
and is then discarded. It is never saved and never leaves your computer.

## What it sends

Nothing. addestroyer has no server, no analytics, no ads of its own and no
tracking. It makes no network requests at all.

## Permissions

- **Access to all websites**: ads can be on any site, so addestroyer has to
  run on every page to find and remove them, and to take the picture
  described above.
- **storage**: keeps the settings and counts listed above on your computer.
- **contextMenus**: adds "Destroy this with addestroyer" to the right-click
  menu.

## Deleting your data

Removing addestroyer from Chrome deletes everything it stored.

## Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new date.
